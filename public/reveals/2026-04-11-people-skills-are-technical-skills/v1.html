Tenet 01 of 7

Communication

The most technically correct message, delivered badly, is still a failed message.

#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
01

Communication

SKILL 1 OF 7
What Is It?
Sending and receiving information clearly — across technical and non-technical audiences, written and verbal.
Doing It Wrong
Optimizing for precision over clarity. Ghosting threads. Over-documenting, under-communicating.
Doing It Right
Adjust your register per audience. A code review explanation ≠ a PM update. Same fact, different message.
Doing It Better
Treat written comms like code — refactor it. Ask for feedback on your Slack messages the way you do on PRs.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Communication · Real-World Scenario
🎬 THE SCENARIO
Sprint planning, Monday morning. You message the team: 'The auth service is broken because the token refresh logic doesn't handle edge cases in the OAuth flow.' Your PM asks the group: 'Is anything blocked?'
⚡ THE TENSION
Nobody answers. The PM assumes things are fine. The auth service stays broken for three days before anyone escalates. You technically communicated — but nothing changed.
✅ THE LESSON
Communication isn't what you sent. It's what was received and acted on. Different audience, different message.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Tenet 02 of 7

Interpersonal Skills

You can be the best engineer on the team and still be the reason good people leave.

#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
02

Interpersonal Skills

SKILL 2 OF 7
What Is It?
Building and maintaining trust, respect, and working relationships with the people who share your codebase.
Doing It Wrong
Optimizing to be right rather than effective. Confusing directness with aggression. Making people feel dumb for asking questions.
Doing It Right
Assume good intent first. That bad architecture decision was made with limited info — exactly like yours have been.
Doing It Better
Know your teammates as people. It makes the hard conversations significantly easier.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Interpersonal Skills · Real-World Scenario
🎬 THE SCENARIO
A teammate leaves a PR comment: "This is the wrong approach entirely." No explanation. No suggestion. Just a rejection. You've spent two days on this feature.
⚡ THE TENSION
You feel defensive. You push back. They double down. The PR sits unmerged for a week while two people have a territory war in the comments. The feature ships late.
✅ THE LESSON
Feedback is a collaborative act. "This is wrong" is an opinion. "Here's why this might cause problems and here's what I'd try instead" is a conversation.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Tenet 03 of 7

Problem Solving

Jumping to solutions is not problem solving. It's solution cosplay.

#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
03

Problem Solving

SKILL 3 OF 7
What Is It?
Identifying root causes, evaluating tradeoffs, and making decisions under uncertainty — with other people, not just alone.
Doing It Wrong
Jumping to implementation. Picking the first valid answer. Solving alone when the problem involves multiple people.
Doing It Right
Define the problem before the solution. "We need a new framework" is already a solution — not a problem statement.
Doing It Better
Bring people in earlier. The person closest to the user knows things sprint planning never surfaces.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Problem Solving · Real-World Scenario
🎬 THE SCENARIO
Staging is throwing 500 errors. Half the team jumps into the codebase. Fixes start appearing in multiple branches simultaneously. Two people debug the same function from different angles.
⚡ THE TENSION
An hour later, someone realizes the deployment pipeline ran the wrong migration. None of the code fixes were needed. All that real engineering effort — wasted.
✅ THE LESSON
Diagnosing together before splitting into solutions is faster than five parallel sprints toward the wrong answer.

Tenet 04 of 7

Time Management

You can't manage time. You can only manage decisions about what you do with it.

#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
04

Time Management

SKILL 4 OF 7
What Is It?
Protecting your time for deep work — not just tasks. Context switching and recovery are real costs that don't show up in estimates.
Doing It Wrong
Saying yes to everything. Treating your calendar like a public park. Estimating in ideal hours, living in actual ones.
Doing It Right
Time-box your work. Treat focus time as non-negotiable — not a luxury you get to when meetings slow down.
Doing It Better
"I'm blocked until Thursday" said Tuesday is very different from "I'm blocked and the deadline is tomorrow."
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Time Management · Real-World Scenario
🎬 THE SCENARIO
You're in three recurring meetings, a Slack thread that's been going 45 minutes, and you just got tagged in a PR. It's 2pm. You haven't written a line of code.
⚡ THE TENSION
By Friday you've worked 50 hours and shipped 20% of what you committed to. You're exhausted. Your team is waiting on you. Your estimates look terrible.
✅ THE LESSON
Your calendar is your architecture. If you wouldn't ship code with no structure, don't ship your week with no structure either.

Tenet 05 of 7

Adaptability

The sprint doesn't care about your plan. Ship anyway.

#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
05

Adaptability

SKILL 5 OF 7
What Is It?
Recognizing when conditions change and updating your approach — without making it a crisis for you or your team.
Doing It Wrong
Treating the original plan like a contract. Letting sunk cost trap you. Taking requirement changes personally.
Doing It Right
Decouple your identity from your decisions. New information changing the plan is engineering — not failure.
Doing It Better
Retros aren't ritual — they're a feedback loop. If nothing changes after every retro, the retro itself is broken.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Adaptability · Real-World Scenario
🎬 THE SCENARIO
Three weeks into a six-week project, the client changes a core requirement. The feature you've been building is now irrelevant. The team is frustrated. Some people want to push back.
⚡ THE TENSION
Two engineers spend two days re-litigating the original spec in Slack instead of pivoting. By the time they accept the change, the new deadline is in jeopardy.
✅ THE LESSON
The engineers who advance fastest aren't the ones with the best original solutions. They're the ones who recover from changes the fastest.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Tenet 06 of 7

Attention to Detail

The bug that costs you the most is always the one you were "pretty sure" about.

#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
06

Attention to Detail

SKILL 6 OF 7
What Is It?
Catching what others miss — and building systems so you catch what you would miss too.
Doing It Wrong
Mistaking speed for efficiency. Shipping fast and fixing "later" — where later quietly becomes never.
Doing It Right
Externalize your attention. Checklists, PR templates, linting — systems beat memory every time.
Doing It Better
Apply detective energy to people too. A conversation that feels off is a bug — reproduce it, isolate it, fix it.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Attention to Detail · Real-World Scenario
🎬 THE SCENARIO
QA signs off on a feature. You deploy to production Friday afternoon. An hour later support tickets start arriving. The date format in the API response is returning year-month-day in one timezone and day-month-year in another.
⚡ THE TENSION
It was in the original spec. You read it. You just didn't test for it. Four hours of incident response, a hotfix, and a Monday post-mortem that was entirely preventable.
✅ THE LESSON
Attention to detail is not a personality trait. It's a system you build because you know you'll miss things without one.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Tenet 07 of 7

Leadership

Leadership is not a title. It starts the moment you have context that someone else doesn't.

#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
07

Leadership

SKILL 7 OF 7
What Is It?
Influencing outcomes — title or not — by sharing knowledge, creating clarity, and making teammates more effective.
Doing It Wrong
Waiting for the title. Hoarding context. Staying quiet in meetings because "it's not your place."
Doing It Right
Mentor without being asked. Document decisions. Speak up when you see a problem — even if you didn't cause it.
Doing It Better
Seek small-stage reps: run the retro, write the post-mortem, present at team sync. Small reps build for the moments that matter.
#OrlandoCodeCamp26@Nerajno | developingdvlpr.com
Leadership · Real-World Scenario
🎬 THE SCENARIO
A mid-sprint standup. Everyone gives their status. Nobody mentions the dependency conflict between two features being built in parallel. It's obvious to you — but you're not the tech lead, so you say nothing.
⚡ THE TENSION
By Thursday, both features are blocked. The tech lead schedules a two-hour unblocking session that was a ten-second conversation away from being unnecessary.
✅ THE LESSON
Leadership is not about rank. It's about responsibility to the shared outcome. If you see the problem and stay quiet, you own the outcome too.
