Create Polished Artifacts

Pillar 01 — Build It @Nerajno

Explain Your Decisions

Documentation is a portfolio piece — not an afterthought

Every technical choice you made has a reason. Write it down. That reasoning is your competitive advantage in interviews, code reviews, and conversations with stakeholders.

Tech Stack Choice

"I chose Vue 3 over React because the Composition API's TypeScript integration matches how I think about component boundaries."

Architecture Decision

"I used Pinia over Vuex because the store is closer to plain TypeScript — less boilerplate, easier to test."

The Learning Journey

"I started with the Options API, hit a wall with complex state, then refactored to Composition API — here's what changed and why."

The Rule

Document your work through blogs, READMEs, or LinkedIn posts. Share your reasoning — not just the outcome. The reasoning is what makes you hireable.

A well-documented tradeoff shows more seniority than perfect code with no explanation.

Pillar 02 — Speak to It @Nerajno

Writing a Winning CFP

The proposal is a sales pitch — for yourself
The Formula

Problem → Your Angle → What They'll Leave With

Real Example

Problem: Developers build great things but can't talk about them.
Angle: Demonstrated competence as a career framework.
Outcome: A portfolio strategy, a speaking playbook, and tools to handle imposter syndrome.

Sound familiar? That's this talk — and it got accepted.

  1. Hook in sentence one. Reviewers read hundreds. Open with the problem, not your bio.
  2. Be specific about the audience. "Developers with 1–3 years experience who want to level up" beats "anyone interested in tech."
  3. Name 3 concrete takeaways. Not vibes — actual things they can do Monday morning.
  4. Match the conference's theme. DevNexus ≠ Connect.Tech ≠ CascadiaJS. Read the CFP guidelines and mirror the language.
  5. Submit early. Submit often. Rejection is data. I track every CFP in CoffeeCircuit.

Frame Your Work as a Story

Humans remember narratives — not bullet lists of features
The Feature List (forgettable)

"I built a Vue 3 app with Pinia state management, Tailwind CSS, Supabase for the backend, and Vitest for testing."

The Story (memorable)

"I was losing track of 30+ conference submissions across spreadsheets and email threads. I built CoffeeCircuit in Vue 3 to fix that — and now it's the tool I demo when someone asks what I can build."

The Story Arc for Any Project

Before: What was the problem or friction?
During: What did you decide — and why that approach?
After: What changed? What did you learn?

Your Story Prompt

Think of the last thing you built. Can you tell me the Before / During / After in 60 seconds? If not — write it down tonight. That story is worth money.

Pillar 03 — Share It @Nerajno

Build in Public — A Weekly Cadence

Consistency beats perfection every single time

You don't need to post every day. You need a system. Here's one that works:

 Monday — Intention

"This week I'm working on ___ because ___." Post it. One sentence. Creates accountability.

 Wednesday — Progress

A screenshot, a code snippet, a problem you hit. "Stuck on X — here's what I tried." That's content.

 Friday — Reflection

"Here's what I shipped, what broke, and what I'd do differently." 3 sentences. Posts like this are portfolio gold.

Why This Works

Over 52 weeks that's 156 micro-posts, ~52 TILs, and a documented record of your growth. Future employers can see your trajectory, not just your final state.

The Compound Effect

One post from 6 months ago about a Vue 3 composable I built still drives traffic to my site today. Build once, earn reach forever.

Start with LinkedIn. Add dev.to. Then your own blog. In that order.

Imposter Syndrome

A psychological phenomenon of feeling like a fraud — even when there's clear evidence of your competence.

How It Shows Up
  • "Everyone else already knows this"
  • "My code isn't good enough to share"
  • "I'm not ready to submit a CFP yet"
  • "I'm just a junior"
  • "What if they ask me something I don't know?"
The Honest Truth

I gave 20+ conference talks while calling myself "Junior~ish." The label didn't stop me. Neither should it stop you.

Its presence often signals high standards — not incompetence. Recognize it. Then go for it anyway.

Pillar 04 — Emotional Wherewithal @Nerajno

The Brag Doc

Your evidence file — write it before you need it

A brag doc is not arrogant. It's a running record of what you shipped, what you fixed, and what you made better — so you're never caught flat-footed at a review.

What Goes In It
  • Features shipped and their business impact
  • Bugs fixed that were costing time or money
  • Things you learned and then taught to someone else
  • Talks given, articles written, PRs merged
  • Feedback received — positive and constructive
When Imposter Syndrome Hits

Open the doc. Read three items. That is the evidence. You cannot argue with a timestamp and a shipped URL.

The Weekly Habit

Friday afternoon. 5 minutes. One entry. Over a year that's 52 data points that tell the story of your growth — in your own words, before anyone else frames it for you.

Tools: Notion, a private GitHub gist, a text file. The format doesn't matter. The habit does.
