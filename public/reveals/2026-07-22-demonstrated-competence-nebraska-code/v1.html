Step 1

Speak to It

Optional: photo of you presenting/whiteboarding a decision

What is it?

Narrating the "Why," Not Just the "What"

Definition

Demonstrated competence = code you shipped + the causal story of why you shipped it that way.

If you've shipped something, you "should" have something worth saying speaking about.

How do we do it wrong?

The Changelog Trap

  • "I built a caching layer." Full stop.
  • No business/learning tie-in — nobody hears what it solved or what you learnt
  • No tradeoffs mentioned — sounds like a feature list, not judgment

The Example — a mistake, not a highlight reel

Here's Where I Got This Wrong

Screenshot or photo: the review/talk/PR where you couldn't explain the "why"

[Speaker note: tell the real one — a review where you froze, a talk that fell flat, a decision you couldn't defend. The point isn't the failure, it's what you did next.]

How do we do it better?

Rehearse It OUT LOUD

Not written. Spoken.

The gap between "I could explain this" and "I did explain this and it landed" is rehearsal.

Turn & Talk — 4 Minutes

The 60-Second Receipt

  1. 1. Pair up with your neighbor.
  2. 2. 60 seconds: explain ONE thing you built — day job, side project, or open source — using Problem → Choice → Tradeoff → Result.
  3. 3. Partner's job: ask "so what did that actually solve?" if the business tie-in gets skipped.
  4. 4. Swap. Go.

Step 4

Imposter Syndrome
Is a Liar

Optional: a quiet, honest portrait — no stock "stressed developer" clichés

What is it?

Doubt That Scales WITH Growth

New level, new doubt.

It's a recurring tax, not a one-time bug.

How do we do it wrong?

Going Quiet at the Worst Time

  • Treating the doubt as evidence you don't belong
  • Instead of evidence you're growing
  • Going quiet exactly when you should be speaking (yes — back to Step 1)

How do we do it good?

Name It Out Loud

Doubt loses power in the open.

Say it to someone else. Not just to yourself, at 2am.

How do we do it better?

Keep a Receipts Log

Literal proof you can reread when the doubt lies to you.

Everything you did tonight — the 60-second receipt, the inventory, the first sentence — that's already the log.
