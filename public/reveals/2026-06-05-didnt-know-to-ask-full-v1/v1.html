The assumption we have to kill

Asking ≠ Failing

Many developers think asking for help is an admission. That it reveals something. That it undermines credibility.

In reality: it usually means you are doing the job well.

Software is complex. Deadlines are real. No one knows everything at once. The developers who pretend otherwise are burning hours — and goodwill.

@Nerajno · #AskBetter
Bigger than you

A team where people ask freely is faster and healthier than one where everyone suffers in silence.

How you ask
shapes team norms —

Psychological safety: if asking feels unsafe on your team, that's a team culture problem — not a personal failing. Name it.

A real technique with a real name

The Rubber
Duck Technique

Explain your problem out loud — to a duck, a blank doc, a voice memo, an AI. Anything that forces articulation.

More often than not: you find the answer mid-explanation.

When you don't? Your explanation becomes your question. The act of writing it is part of solving it.

‹14›
@Nerajno · #AskBetter
2026 update

AI as First-Line
Resource

Use AI for
  • Decoding error messages
  • Unfamiliar APIs / patterns
  • Getting a starting point
  • Rubber duck at scale
Escalate to a human when
  • AI's fix didn't work
  • Context-specific to your repo
  • Judgment call required
  • Stakes are too high to guess
‹15›
@Nerajno · #AskBetter
Before any ask

Search First

  • Paste the exact error into Google
  • Check official docs + GitHub issues for the library
  • Stack Overflow — yes, still
  • Claude / Copilot / ChatGPT for explanation + starting point

Showing you searched makes your question significantly stronger. It's a signal of respect.

Year 0–1: the 5-part structure

A Strong Question
Includes:

  1. What are you trying to do?
  2. What happened instead?
  3. What have you already tried?
  4. What do you think is wrong?
  5. What kind of help do you need right now?

The difference in practice

❌ vs ✅

WEAK

"My app is broken."

STRONG

"Getting a 422 on form submit in my Vue app. Confirmed payload in the network tab. Tried validating each field manually. Still can't tell which the server rejects. Can you help me narrow down the field?"

Year 1–3 upgrade

Ask Earlier,
More Precisely

"I think the bug is in the state update — component renders correctly before the async call returns. Moved the update to a separate function, same result. Does this look like a timing problem?"

Shows reasoning, not frustration. Invites collaboration. Doesn't hand off the problem.

Practical tips · Years 0–3

The Habits That
Compound

Name your hypothesis

"I think it might be X" gives direction — confirm or redirect.

Async channels first

Slack > interrupting focus. Write it like you'd say it in person.

Share a repro

StackBlitz, CodeSandbox, Gist. Smallest version that still breaks.

Close the loop

"Turned out it was a race condition in the effect cleanup." Always.

‹24›
@Nerajno · #AskBetter
Practical tips · Years 3–5

Managing Time
and Blockers

Set a time limit

30–45 min of spinning = time to ask. Thrashing ≠ independence.

Bring a proposed solution

"I'm thinking X — does that seem right?" is faster than "I don't know."

Know when to escalate

"This might need someone with deeper DB layer knowledge. I've gotten to X."

Async before screenshare

Screenshare is a premium ask of someone's focus. Use it when truly needed.

The tradeoff ask · Years 5–10

Ask About
Decisions

"I've narrowed this to two options. Option A is faster but adds technical debt. Option B fits the architecture better but takes longer. I'd like your perspective on which tradeoff makes sense for this release."

Shows ownership. Frames the problem. Invites help making a decision — not a rescue.

Practical tips · Years 5–10

Asking as
Leadership

Bring context

Your manager / principal may not have your feature's full background. Brief them.

Know when to decide

Seeking genuine input vs. seeking validation — be honest with yourself.

Ask in public

Team channels normalize the behavior. Senior devs set the example.

Write it down

RFC / short doc > one-off question at this level. Creates a record. Scales the ask.

‹33›
@Nerajno · #AskBetter
The default posture

Async-First
Principles

  • Write questions specific enough that no clarification is needed first
  • Respect that the answer may come in 2 hours, not 2 minutes
  • Async creates a written record — others find it later

Asking well + documenting answers = the same culture.

‹34›
@Nerajno · #AskBetter
Underrated tool

Loom is a
Valid Ask

A 2-minute screen recording — showing the bug, explaining what you've tried, asking one specific question — can outperform a long Slack message.

Still async. Lower friction. Higher clarity. Creates a record.

Add it to your toolkit. Especially useful for visual bugs or flow-level issues that are hard to describe in text.

‹35›
@Nerajno · #AskBetter
When to break the rule

When Sync Is
the Right Call

  • Production is down
  • A deadline is imminent
  • Too nuanced to describe without losing critical context
  • Multiple people need the same context at the same time

Everything else: async first. Always try the message before the call.

‹36›
@Nerajno · #AskBetter
Works at every level · every year

The Formula

  1. What am I trying to do?
  2. What exactly is blocking me?
  3. What have I already tried?
  4. What do I think is happening?
  5. What kind of help do I need?
  6. What will I do next if this doesn't work?

#6 is underrated. It signals maturity and keeps the conversation moving even if the help doesn't fully land.

‹37›
@Nerajno · #AskBetter
Do stuff… sorry… just don't do this

Common Mistakes

  • Asking too early. No prep forces the other person to start from zero.
  • Waiting too long. 3 hrs of thrashing ≠ independence. It's waste.
  • Being vague. "It doesn't work" is not a question.
  • The handoff ask. "Please fix this" ≠ "here's where I'm stuck."
  • Not closing the loop. When someone helps you — follow up. Always.

Speaker Backup — Core Arc + Key Points

Core thesis: Asking for help is a skill that evolves across a full career — not a binary of needing help vs. not.

Years 0–1: Clarity. Describe the problem so someone else can understand it. Five-part structure: what I'm trying to do, what happened, what I tried, what I think, what I need.

Years 1–3: Precision + timing. Ask earlier. Name your hypothesis. Route wisely. Close the loop.

Years 3–5: Efficiency. Time-box to 30–45 min. Bring a proposed solution. Know when to escalate. Default async.

Years 5–10: Leadership. Frame tradeoffs. Ask in public. Write RFCs. Know when to decide alone. Ask up vs. across.

Psych safety: If asking feels unsafe, that's a team problem — name it.
