Act 1

GEO Explained

Three eras. One fundamental goal.

Let's understand how we got here — and what makes GEO genuinely different.

Three Eras of Search

Era 1 — 1990s–now

SEO

Rank higher.
Get clicked.

Era 2 — 2015–now

AEO

Be the answer.
Skip the click.

Era 3 — NOW

GEO

Be cited by AI.
Influence without a visit.

A single query can hit all three simultaneously. Optimize for only one and you're invisible in the others.

These are layers, not replacements. SEO didn't die when AEO emerged. They stack. You're here because GEO is the new layer most developers are missing.

SEO — How do I rank?

SEO targets search engines. The output is a ranked blue link. You win when the user clicks through.

KEY LEVERS:

  • Keywords & backlinks & domain authority
  • Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS)
  • Crawlability, sitemaps, canonical URLs

Still essential for buying-intent and local queries — where AI Overviews appear least often.

drop in clicks to #1 rank
when AI Overview appears

Ahrefs analysis, ~300K keywords

~48%

of Google queries now
show an AI Overview

Estimated; varies by query type

AEO — How do I become the answer?

AEO targets the answer box that appears above the links. You win when Google or Siri reads your content aloud — no click required.

KEY LEVERS:

  • Schema markup: FAQPage, HowTo, Speakable
  • Direct answer in the first sentence
  • Question-style H2s that match how people ask

Featured Snippet

Your words, Google's voice.
User never visits your site.

Zero-click Result

Your brand gets read.
Your URL may not.

AEO is the middle child most developers skip entirely. Schema markup is table stakes for modern search.

GEO — How does AI decide what to cite?

GEO targets AI-native engines. They don't return links — they synthesize a response and cite their sources. You win by being one of them.

KEY LEVERS:

  • Entity authority — who you are, clearly defined
  • Structured data LLM crawlers can parse
  • Self-contained, citable paragraphs

Failure mode: AI hallucinates your data, or ignores you entirely.

AI TOOLS TARGETED:

ChatGPT Search
Perplexity AI
Google AI Overviews

REAL EXAMPLE:

Perplexity cites Smashing Magazine as the source for "how to structure a Vue 3 component" — even though hundreds of sites cover the topic — because their article has Person schema, structured FAQ, and explicit author identity.

The failure mode is brutal: the AI hallucinates your data, gets facts wrong, or ignores you entirely. The win is being the source it trusts. Smashing Magazine is a good concrete example — they didn't do anything magic, they just structured their content correctly.

How AI Engines Actually Work

WHAT THEY LOOK FOR:

Semantic HTML — article, section, h1–h6 signals content structure

JSON-LD Schema — machine-readable metadata LLM crawlers prioritize

Entity clarity — consistent, cross-referenced author identity

✗ KILLS CITABILITY:

  • "As mentioned above…"
  • Dangling pronouns ("it," "this," "they")
  • Context-dependent sentences
  • No author • No schema

Each paragraph must make sense extracted from context. AI can't scroll back up.

Think of an LLM crawler as a very fast, very literal reader who can't scroll back up. "As I mentioned above" is a broken citation.

The 3 Core Pillars of GEO

Every GEO optimization traces back to one of these three.

Semantic HTML
Structure

Proper heading hierarchy, landmark elements, meaningful markup

Explicit Content
Formatting

Self-contained paragraphs, direct answers, citable writing

Structured Data
Markup

JSON-LD schema that LLM crawlers parse and trust

These pillars also strengthen AEO and SEO — it's a rising tide that lifts all three.

Pillar 1 — Semantic HTML Structure

Why it matters: HTML tags are meaning signals. AI crawlers weight <article> and <h1> differently than <div class="title">. Same visual output — completely different machine-readable signal.

✗ AI-INVISIBLE

<div class="blog-post">
  <div class="title">
    My Tutorial
  </div>
  <div class="body">
    <div>Step one...</div>
    <div>Step two...</div>
  </div>
</div>

✓ GEO-READY

<article>
  <header>
    <h1>How to Handle State
        in Vue 3</h1>
    <time datetime="2026-01-15">
      Jan 2026</time>
    <address>
      By Nerando Johnson
    </address>
  </header>
  <section>
    <h2>Why Pinia Over Vuex</h2>
    <p>Pinia is the recommended...</p>
  </section>
</article>

Pillar 2 — Write for Humans, Structure for AI

✗ KILLS AI DISCOVERABILITY

  • "As mentioned above, this approach…"
  • "We looked at this earlier, but…"
  • "It depends on what you're building."
  • "See the next section for details."

✓ CITABLE / SELF-CONTAINED

  • Every paragraph answers a specific question
  • Subject always explicit — no dangling pronouns
  • Direct assertions: "Pinia is the official Vue 3 state manager"
  • Definitions included inline, not referenced elsewhere

The Litmus Test:

"If someone extracted just this paragraph and read it with no context, would it still make complete sense?"

If no → rewrite it. AI will skip it.

Pillar 3 — Structured Data Markup

JSON-LD is the language of structured data. It lives in your <head> and tells AI crawlers exactly what your content is.

3 SCHEMA TYPES YOU NEED:

BlogPosting — for every article, tutorial, and blog entry

FAQPage — maps directly to how users query AI platforms

Person — establishes you as a known, citable entity

WHY JSON-LD?

  • Doesn't pollute your HTML
  • Easy to add in Astro/Vue <head>
  • Google + LLM crawlers parse it
  • Validated by Rich Results Test

[ JSON-LD in DevTools ]

JSON-LD — BlogPosting Schema

Why it matters: This invisible snippet in your <head> tells AI crawlers what the page is, who wrote it, and when — the three things needed to cite you with confidence. Your readers never see it; crawlers can't ignore it.

Add this inside a <script type="application/ld+json"> tag in your page <head>:

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "BlogPosting",
  "headline": "How to Handle State Management in Vue 3 with Pinia",
  "author": {
    "@type": "Person",
    "name": "Nerando Johnson",
    "url": "https://developingdvlpr.com",
    "sameAs": [
      "https://linkedin.com/in/nerajno",
      "https://bsky.app/profile/nerajno.bsky.social"
    ]
  },
  "datePublished": "2026-01-15",
  "dateModified": "2026-02-01",
  "description": "A complete guide to Vue 3 state management using Pinia...",
  "url": "https://developingdvlpr.com/blog/vue3-pinia-state",
  "keywords": ["Vue 3", "Pinia", "state management", "frontend"]
}

author.sameAs is your entity signal — it links your identity across platforms. AI knowledge graphs use this to confirm you are who you say you are.

The sameAs array is the secret weapon. AI knowledge graphs see "Nerando Johnson on site X = Nerando Johnson on site Y" and build entity authority.

JSON-LD — FAQPage Schema

Why it matters: FAQ schema maps directly to conversational AI queries. When someone asks ChatGPT "What's the difference between Pinia and Vuex?", a well-structured FAQPage is exactly what it looks for to cite.

Each question + answer pair becomes a citable unit:

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [{
    "@type": "Question",
    "name": "What is the difference between Pinia and Vuex in Vue 3?",
    "acceptedAnswer": {
      "@type": "Answer",
      "text": "Pinia is the official state management library for Vue 3,
               replacing Vuex. Pinia offers a simpler API, better TypeScript
               support, and native DevTools integration. Unlike Vuex,
               Pinia uses a store-per-feature pattern with no mutations."
    }
  }]
}

Each answer must be fully self-contained. Write it as a direct response — no "see our full guide."

Match questions to real queries people ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI.

JSON-LD — Person Schema

Why it matters: This makes you a named entity in the AI knowledge graph — not just an anonymous page author. Without it, AI engines can't confidently attribute expertise to you across multiple articles and platforms.

Add once to your portfolio homepage. It anchors your identity everywhere:

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Person",
  "name": "Nerando Johnson",
  "alternateName": "Nerajno",
  "url": "https://developingdvlpr.com",
  "jobTitle": "Frontend Software Engineer",
  "knowsAbout": ["Vue 3", "Astro", "TypeScript", "GEO", "Web Accessibility"],
  "worksFor": { "@type": "Organization", "name": "Hygiena" },
  "sameAs": [
    "https://linkedin.com/in/nerajno",
    "https://github.com/nerajno",
    "https://bsky.app/profile/nerajno.bsky.social"
  ],
  "description": "Frontend engineer in Atlanta, GA specializing in Vue 3,
                  Astro, and web accessibility."
}

knowsAbout is your expertise signal. AI uses this to decide when to cite you on a given topic.

Astro Component — Before & After

Why it matters: The visual output of this component is identical before and after. The machine-readable output is completely different. That's the GEO design principle: structure for machines, write for humans.

✗ BEFORE (AI-invisible)

---
const { title, content, date }
  = Astro.props
---
<div class="post">
  <div class="post-header">
    <h1>{ title }</h1>
    <span>{ date }</span>
  </div>
  <div class="post-body">
    <Fragment set:html={content}/>
  </div>
</div>

No semantic tags

No author signal

No schema

✓ AFTER (GEO-ready)

---
const { title, content, date,
        description, tags }
  = Astro.props
---
<script type="application/ld+json"> <!-- ① -->
  {JSON.stringify({
    "@type": "BlogPosting",
    "headline": title,
    "author": {           <!-- ② -->
      "@type": "Person",
      "name": "Nerando Johnson"
    },
    "datePublished": date <!-- ③ -->
  })}
</script>
<article>              <!-- ① semantic -->
  <header>
    <h1>{ title }</h1>
    <time datetime={date}>{ date }</time>
    <address>By Nerando Johnson</address>
  </header>
  <section>
    <Fragment set:html={content}/>
  </section>
</article>

① JSON-LD schema • ② named author • ③ date
Three additions. Zero visual change. Full AI citability.

The visible output barely changes. The machine-readable output changes dramatically. That's the GEO design principle: structure for machines, write for humans.

Building AI-Friendly FAQ Sections

SEMANTIC FAQ HTML:

<section aria-label="FAQ">
  <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

  <details>
    <summary>
      What is Vue 3 Composition API?
    </summary>
    <p>
      The Vue 3 Composition API is a set of
      additive, function-based APIs that
      allow flexible composition of logic.
      Unlike Options API, it organizes code
      by feature rather than option type.
    </p>
  </details>
</section>

QUESTION WRITING RULES:

1

Start with What, How, Why, When, Which — mirrors conversational AI queries

2

Write answers that work without reading the question first

3

Aim for 3–6 sentences per answer — citable but not skippable

Advanced — Entity Optimization

An entity is an unambiguous, well-defined concept the AI knowledge graph recognizes and trusts.

BUILDING YOUR ENTITY:

Consistent name + handle across all platforms (@nerajno everywhere)

Cross-referencing — your blog cites your talks; your talks mention your blog

Citations from trusted sources — Sessionize, Vue Mastery, conference sites

TOPIC CLUSTER STRATEGY:

  • 5+ articles on Vue 3 routing → you become an authority on Vue routing to AI
  • Link articles within the cluster to each other
  • Add a hub/index page that collects them all
  • Reinforce with matching JSON-LD keywords
